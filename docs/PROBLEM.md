# Problem Validation

## The hospital monitor problem

Open Bloomberg, MetaTrader, IBKR, even TradingView for the first time. The screen looks like a hospital monitor. Twelve panels, a hundred numbers, candlesticks doing things you don't understand, an order ticket asking you to commit real money before you've understood what any of it means.

The interface assumes you already know.

That's why **74% of new retail traders quit within their first 90 days** ([Brokerchooser, 2024](https://brokerchooser.com/education/research/why-traders-fail)). It's not because they're stupid. It's because the on-ramp is a cliff.

## What people actually do instead

- Watch YouTube videos. Passive. No skin in the game.
- Read PDF guides. Static. No feedback loop.
- Open a paper-trading account. Looks like the cliff but with fake money. They quit anyway.
- Copy-trade on a wallet tracker. Zero learning, all hope.

None of these surfaces let you **practice the act of trading** in a way that:

1. **Builds intuition** — your hand learns where the bracket goes, where the stop sits, what fast moves feel like.
2. **Reaches the same primitive** — when you "graduate," your bracket on Solana mainnet is the same bracket you placed a thousand times in the game.
3. **Is actually fun** — the skill loop pulls you back tomorrow.

## The crypto-native angle

Two structural shifts make 2026 the year:

1. **Hyperliquid + Phantom flipped the wallet UX.** A 22-year-old can fund a Phantom wallet and place a perp on Hyperliquid in under 4 minutes. The infrastructure barrier is gone. The skill barrier remains.
2. **Memecoin season trained 8M+ wallets to swap on-chain.** They have wallets. They have liquidity. They don't have a way to learn anything beyond "press buy."

The conversion funnel from "casual swapper" to "actual trader who places brackets and manages risk" is broken. Nobody owns it.
